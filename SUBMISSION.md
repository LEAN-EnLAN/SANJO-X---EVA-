## High-level summary
Space travel is turning commercial, but health in space still depends on a call to Earth that can take many minutes each way, with no doctor on board. Eva is a Super Intelligence health copilot for everyone on board, crew and passengers alike.

She lives on a rugged two-module wristband and works with the ship's emergency station. In a cardiac arrest, she sends the nearest passenger to start CPR and paces it, lights the AED compartment at the station, and records every step. In a solar particle event, she guides each person to the shelter. Every day, she watches vitals against your own baseline and listens when you feel alone.

It all runs on board, offline, with a signed record that Earth receives and acknowledges. Eva gives diagnostic support only: the AED decides the shock.

## Project details
**Wristband.** A sensor base sits on the skin and reads heart rate, SpO₂, skin temperature, EDA and movement, with a 30 s ECG on demand (two fingers of the same hand on two metal pads). A screen module snaps on with magnets and lifts off with one hand. In suit mode the base stays under the suit and keeps sending on its own battery.

**Network.** Wristbands talk LoRa and relay for each other as a mesh. The ship hub hosts the Super Intelligence and stores everything. Phones and the emergency station are clients. If the hub is down, the alarm goes straight from the wristband to the station.

**Super Intelligence.** It is built from MedGemma, Google's open medical model, plus our space-medicine layer and a strict topic guard: space and health only, and no treatment or shock decisions. Every case follows the 10-second rule: once Eva calls, everyone knows what to do, where and how.

**Records.** Every event is signed on board (an Ed25519 hash chain), queued, and sent when the link allows. The ground instance receives and acknowledges each record.

**What we built.**
- A working app as one Rust binary: passenger PWA, wristband mode, crew dashboard, emergency station screen and ground instance, with 152 automated tests.
- An Android APK with online, offline and hub modes.
- A hosted demo with live voice, photo and chat.
- A hosted backend with row-level security.
- A landing page, a research hub, a pitch and a 30 s video.

**Scalability.** Eva is the control interface and assistant, and modules plug into it: new health devices, emergency and catastrophe response, information, entertainment and learning. It is the computer of space, from tourist flights to commercial stations and future deep-space bases.

## NASA data
- NASA Open Science Data Repository: Inspiration4 metabolic panel (OSD-575) and complete blood count (OSD-569). We used them for baselines and for our own analysis of out-of-range values.
- NASA DONKI (space weather events) and NOAA GOES proton flux, for the solar particle event flow.
- NASA Human Health and Performance data sharing: https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/hhp/human-health-and-performance-data-sharing/
- NASA 3D Resources (Sun model on the landing).
- Prior work that inspired us: NASA's ARCHeR study on Artemis II, with the ActLumus wrist actigraph.

## Use of Super Intelligence tools
- **Images and video.** The concept images and the video footage were generated by the team with Super Intelligence image and video models, then edited by us. They show no real people and no NASA insignia, and they are declared in `landing/a/AI-NOTICE.md`.
- **Voices.** The video's voices were made with text-to-speech models.
- **Inside the product.** Eva uses language, speech and vision models through Groq: an open-weight chat model, Whisper for speech-to-text, Orpheus for speech and a vision model, all behind our topic guard.
- **Development.** Coding assistants helped write and test the code and documents.
- **What we decided ourselves.** All product decisions, medical framing and claims were made and checked by the team. Every number on the slides has a verifiable source.
