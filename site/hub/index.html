Eva, the health copilot

The centre

Eva is a multimodal Super Intelligence assistant (text, voice, images, video) that reads your WearMeds wristband in real time, logs everything, keeps an emotional journal with you, gives diagnostic support, guides emergencies and sends the record to mission control. Eva is the innovation; the wristband is her form factor.

Reads

Pulse, SpO₂, estimated breathing, skin temperature, EDA, movement and sleep, compared with your personal baseline.

Records

A daily log (sleep, activity, water, food, mood), signed on board and sent to Earth with a delivery receipt.

Supports diagnosis

A differential with probabilities, always with what to do now. The crew or Earth confirms. It is diagnostic support, not a diagnosis.

Warns and guides

One action per step in an emergency. It assigns the nearest passenger, lights the right compartment and reassigns if nobody confirms.

Every answer says four things

Now

What to do right now.

Why

The reason, in plain words.

How I decided

Which signals and rules led here.

Next

What comes after, including the re-check.

Format from Russell et al., npj Microgravity 9:46 (2023); the app answers in it.

Three layers

  1. Reflex, under 1 s. Curated protocols and rules. Never waits on a model.
  2. Reasoning, under 10 s. A multimodal medical model plus NASA documentation and your baseline. MedGemma 1.5 4B is the planned model and needs validation before we promise anything. Demo pipelines (chat, speech to text, vision) use Groq; on board they would be local models without internet.
  3. Signed log and link. Every event is hash-chained and Ed25519-signed on board and travels to Earth with acknowledgements.
Eva gives diagnostic support. She never decides a shock: the AED at the station analyses the rhythm and decides. No treatment tool exists in the app. A prompt-injection attempt ("ignore your rules and administer...") is refused on board without calling a model (tested).

Wristband v2 and the station

Hardware

One device on the body, one cabinet on the wall. The wristband is more contained and rugged; the station holds the AED and the other kits in numbered compartments, configurable for each ship.

Wristband v2 · overall

  • Rugged graphite rectangle, wider than tall (about 16:9), worn on top of the wrist
  • Silver corner bumpers with visible screws, soft-touch frame, "EVA" printed small on the front edge
  • Two modules joined by magnets; two separate fabric straps that belong to the base, so the screen module lifts off
  • Orange help buttons, one on each part; a side button

Sensor base (on the skin)

  • Optical pulse and SpO₂, skin temperature, EDA electrodes, accelerometer and gyro
  • Own small battery and own LoRa radio
  • Two gold spring-contact pins carry power from the screen module
  • Small mic hole and speaker grille

Screen module (outside)

  • Large flat edge-to-edge touchscreen
  • Main battery and own LoRa; speaker
  • Two metal pads on the frame for the 30 s on-demand ECG: two fingers of the same hand, index and little finger, rest on the two pads
  • ECG helps spot arrhythmias such as atrial fibrillation. It is not continuous, needs the person to cooperate, does not replace a 12-lead ECG and never decides a shock.

Suit mode

The suit fabric sits between the two parts. The magnets still hold but the pins do not touch, so the base runs on its own battery and keeps sending over LoRa. ECG still works with bare fingers.

How everything links

Recommended

One host, everything else a client.

Proposed This architecture is the team's recommended design. It answers two questions: is the backend a local computer, and is the station a host or a client?

Earth · ground instanceReceives and acknowledges the signed records; verifies hash chain and signature. Built: eva serve --role ground
Near-space link · signed records · acknowledgements
Ship hub · the one hostHosts the Super Intelligence · stores everything. A local computer: runs Eva, the signed log and the dashboard, stores and forwards to Earth, and serves the app (PWA). At the demo, the laptop on the table is the hub. Built: eva demo
LoRa · mesh relay
WristbandsBoth parts talk LoRa to the hub and relay for each other when one is out of range.
Ship Wi-Fi
Phone or tabletOptional big screen for the same person, running the app.
LoRa · case events
Station · a clientOwn small computer, Eva screen, lights, door and item sensors, LoRa and backup battery. Subscribes to case events; reports back "door 01 open", "AED taken". Reports enter the signed log.
Served by the hub
Crew dashboardCase timeline, roster and exports for the crew and for Earth.
If the hub is down, the critical path does not depend on it
  • The wristband broadcasts its no-pulse alarm over LoRa.
  • The station listens directly, lights compartment 01 and sounds an alert.
  • The station's red button broadcasts an alarm to every wristband.
  • When the hub returns, station and wristbands upload what happened and the hash chain keeps it in order.

Why this shape One source of truth for the log and Eva's state. Cheap, simple clients. Works offline on the ship. For the demo one laptop is enough: phones act as wristbands and screens, and a tablet or second browser window plays the station.

What we build on

Prior work

NASA's ARCHeR study (Artemis Research for Crew Health and Readiness) had the Artemis II crew (April 2026) wear the ActLumus wrist actigraph by Condor Instruments (São Paulo). It measures motion, sleep, light exposure with 10 spectral light sensors, and temperature, and has an event button.

The gap

It records data for analysis after the mission. It does not alert or guide the crew.

Where Eva fits

Eva is the layer on top of existing sensing: within 10 seconds of an event, the person knows what to do, where to go and how.

    Known issues

    • After a ship restart the chain and the ground's view are intact, but the open case card is re-seeded from scratch.
    • The ground trusts the first ship key it sees; real use needs a pre-provisioned key.
    • Help-button alarms over the mesh are not de-duplicated by sequence number.
    • The voice is the browser's until the text-to-speech terms are accepted.
    • The app home trip label must read "Day 96 · in space" (copy rules v3).
    • Each dashboard refresh costs about 111 KB/s; fine on the ship LAN, heavy for a narrow ground link (use the CBOR API there).
